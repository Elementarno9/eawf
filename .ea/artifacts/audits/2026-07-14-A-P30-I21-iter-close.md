# A-P30-I21 — iter-close evaluation: validation-repair, spawn/campaign correctness, and the v0.6.0 ship gate

## Summary

Iter P30-I21 is the validation-repair pass: it corrects the autopilot spawn, the research campaign, and the cockpit against what the live v0.6.0 validation actually found, and it carries the phase's re-close vehicle. A fresh-context auditor re-read its waves against their typed criteria and, because this is the last gate before the v0.6.0 PR, also re-checked the release pre-flight [1].

Verdict: **minor** (pass-with-followups), **no ship blockers**. The ledger is clean: all 35 closed waves pin a commit SHA that is an ancestor of HEAD and whose subject carries its own wave prefix — verified rather than assumed. Eleven of eleven waves in the correctness cluster were deep-verified against the shipped code; nine hold cleanly. The two findings that mattered were repaired inside the iter (W37, W38) rather than deferred.

## Evidence

- **The release pre-flight passes on every leg.** `__version__` is `0.6.0` against a last release of `v0.5.4`; the `## [0.6.0]` changelog section carries bullets and a migration note for all eleven schema edges between the two (1.8 through 1.19), each naming a migration step that exists in the code; and the drafted phase-close subject was run through the real lint — it exits zero, while the fused `(audit=..., release=...)` shape and a `v`-less `(release=0.6.0)` both hard-reject. The tag workflow's extraction regex matches the drafted subject and does NOT match the fused shape, so the lint and the workflow agree on what a taggable subject looks like [2].
- **The changelog gate was falsified, not merely run.** Deleting a migration note reddens it; mangling the heading reddens it. The gate derives its required edge list from the persisted `schema_version` rather than hardcoding it, which is what closes the hole that let five edges reach the ship gate undocumented [3].
- **The strongest criterion evidence is adversarial.** W29's guard seeds a *foreign* claim-time baseline (five million input tokens, twelve dollars) and proves the headless spawn replaces rather than differences against it, driving the real dispatch and the real close path — not a mock of the path being proven. W01's sandbox carve-out is enforced at the kernel: the test executes a real `sandbox-exec` and asserts an outside-cwd write is blocked while an inside-cwd write lands [4].
- **One wave beat its own criterion.** W30's criterion names an `unwrap_result_envelope` helper; the code instead routes through `terminal_result_envelope`, because the named helper would have returned only the inner prose and dropped the `usage` block. The deviation is in the right direction and the outcome is better than the text asked for.
- **Two findings, both repaired in-iter.** The audit found exactly one idle contract — `read_campaign_cost` had zero production callers, so the campaign cost centre W15 created was queryable only from a test. W37 wires it into `eawf research status`. It also found two statements that had gone false: the run-complete log hardcoded `terminal=converged` even when the convergence flip was skipped, so an operator cancel mid-run was logged as a convergence that never happened; and two adapter docstrings still described the pre-`stream-json` default. W38 retires both, and its guard drives a real mid-run cancel through the round runner [5].
