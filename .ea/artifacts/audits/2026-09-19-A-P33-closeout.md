# P33 closeout audit

## Summary

Phase P33 ran one iter of one hundred waves - ninety-nine planned and one appended when the phase gauntlet caught a late regression - and delivered the epoch-2 native stack together with a release machinery that can walk a checkpoint to BAKED. Epoch-2 authority is fenced to disposable canaries, every epoch-2 mutation commits through one seven-step daemon transaction, terminal records compact into append-only ledgers, and the Track / Milestone / Batch / Task / Run lifecycle, the provider contract with its conformance runner, the semantic-tool gateway and the git worktree leases all sit on that contract. `REL-0.7.0.dev2` was walked through publish, reconcile and observe until it baked, with PyPI, npm and GitHub each read back live against the frozen manifest.

The verdict is **minor**. The five-slice review pass on the phase PR returned two blockers and roughly a dozen majors; both blockers were repaired inside the phase as `P33-I01-W101` before close, and the majors are recorded with their evidence and carried to P34, so the recorded verdict follows the same rule P32's did - repaired in phase, or filed with evidence. What did not land - the `0.7.0.dev3` cut - did not land by decision rather than by failure, and the decision is pinned by a test.

The CI-mirror gauntlet on the final head reads clean in one pass: pre-commit, `deptry` and `mypy` over 1,074 source files are clean; the parallel leg passes 25,713 tests with 74 skipped and one expected failure; the TUI render snapshots, the serial perf timing budgets and the telemetry-producer assertion pass; the per-package coverage ratchet and the overall coverage floor pass against a report generated in that same run; and the snapshot-pairing and strict prose gates are clean. The single red was the phase-branch backup assertion, which is red by construction until the branch is pushed and passes once it is.

## What the audit found

**Seven surfaces are built, tested, and unreachable from production, in six groups.** The shared root cause is not a missing dispatcher. A caller exists for most of them and stops short; what is missing is that **no producer writes the records those surfaces read**, so nothing in the tree can assemble the request halves the verbs require. The groups are the budget meter, the per-Run MCP server configuration, epoch-2 record admission, candidate submission, the delivery verb family, and console native mode. Six of the seven were declared unprompted by the executor that built them, in answer to a standing question about whether a production caller exists. The seventh - the console - nobody declared, because no single wave owned it.

That asymmetry is the phase's most useful process finding. Every wave asked whether its own thing was wired and answered honestly; no wave asked whether the thing holding all their things was wired, because no wave owned it. The per-wave producer question is necessary and was not sufficient, and its counterpart has to be asked once per subsystem.

**Zero of the seven native verbs have a working production caller.** The three lifecycle skills pass the skill's own slash-prefixed name into a parameter typed as an uppercase-anchored principal key, so every skill-originated call is refused at the parameter boundary before it reaches the tree. Called with an operator principal the same verbs accept the request shape and refuse on domain facts instead: the verbs work, the skills cannot reach them. An earlier report to the operator claimed those skills were the native verb family's first production callers; that claim was relayed as fact and is wrong, and the correction is recorded here rather than left standing.

**Four live gates cannot fail.** Three are in `tools/idle_contract_gate.py`: a row counter whose entire body returns true, wired at three call sites, so any row using it counts every record unconditionally; a helpers-wired check that proves two symbols are importable and nothing about whether the ratchet it guards ever runs against real coverage output; and a no-asserting-test contract whose whole mechanism is a word-boundary regex over the raw text of any file under `tests/`, so the symbol appearing in a comment or a docstring discharges it. The fourth is in `tools/coverage_gate.py`, which defines, documents and unit-tests two report-freshness helpers that `main()` never calls - measured during this close, the gate printed its full package table and passed at exit zero against a `coverage.xml` two days older than HEAD. A gate that cannot red is worse than no gate, because it reads as coverage; all four are filed for P34.

The fourth case is this phase's characteristic defect reaching the tooling layer rather than a product surface. A producerless seam in the product leaves a feature inert; a producerless seam in a gate converts a missing check into a passing one. The sharpest detail is that the second gate exists to prove that this very module's helpers are wired, and it checks the two that are wired while the two that are not sit untouched beside them in the same file.

**The pre-ship audit found five defects, all repaired before close.** They are carried in the bake commit together with the dev2 walk and the corrected phase record.

## What the ship pass repaired

The phase PR was reviewed in five slices, each over a disjoint part of the source diff. It returned two blockers and roughly a dozen majors. Both blockers are in the same place and were repaired here as `P33-I01-W101`; the majors are recorded below and carried to P34.

**Both blockers were a tool-authority widening on the native dispatch path.** The first: the two native launchers forwarded a compiled spec's `tool_policy.deny` into the spawn path's ambient deny-list. Those are different vocabularies - the policy holds semantic ids and the deny-list holds ambient names - and the codex lane renders a deny as its inverted allowlist, so a name it does not recognise subtracts nothing and the child is granted every ambient tool. That is strictly more authority than the empty deny-list, which emits no override at all, so a denial made the child **more** permissive than no denial. The id grammar is anchored lowercase, so no value the policy field can legally hold was ever expressible as an ambient name: the forward had no correct input rather than merely a wrong one. The second: `seal_capsule` took the tool grants and denials from the caller's request while taking every other enforcement field from the spec, so a caller could seal back exactly the authority the least-authority merge had withdrawn - and the capsule, not the spec, is what the semantic gateway admits calls against.

Both are witnessed by their own docstrings. `seal_capsule` says every enforcement field is taken from the spec; `CapsuleRequest` says a caller cannot hand a worker authority the compiler did not resolve; the codex helper says a deny cannot silently pass through to the child's effective grant. Each sentence was false for the two fields authority is enforced on.

**Neither was reachable from production, which is why this closed as a guard rather than a redesign.** The only consumer of either native launcher is the native dispatch module, and the only entry to that is the dispatch verb, which no shipped entry point reaches - the same producerless condition this phase documents elsewhere. Nothing live was exposed. The seams still had to close before the native canary checkpoint, because that checkpoint is the first thing that exercises them.

**The repair is fail-closed at both boundaries rather than a translation.** A launcher refuses a denial that names no ambient tool; sealing refuses a capsule more permissive than the merged policy while still admitting one equal to it or stricter. Each guard ships a test proving it reds: removing them was run, not assumed, and failed exactly the capsule-drift refusals and the launcher wiring assertions. The widening mechanism itself is pinned alongside the refusal, so a future change that re-enables the forward reds on the mechanism rather than on a message.

**W101 closed with zero gate receipts, and that should be said plainly.** It carries a criteria-floor waiver and a runtime waiver: it was authored in the main checkout at phase close rather than by a dispatched agent, so there is no runtime capture, and its criteria are legacy strings with no typed gates behind them. The evidence for it is the two committed test modules, their fire-proof run, and the 4,552-test runtime scope that stays green - evidence produced by hand rather than by the close machinery. This is the same shape P32's closeout audit named when it found waves closing with required gates and no receipts. It is defensible here because the proof exists and is committed; it is not defensible as a habit.

**Exactly one finding of the whole review is reachable from a shipped entry point today, and it is filed rather than fixed.** `commit_worktree_state` writes the full state payload and runs the regression refusal and the write note, but never the leak refusal that every sibling writer runs - the two daemon paths, the CLI fallback transaction, the epoch-2 transaction and the plan-apply write all call it, and this one does not. It is reached from `wave land`, `track sync` and `dispatch pause`, so a landed wave carrying a home-anchored path reaches committed state with the gate never consulted. It is filed as B156 rather than repaired here for two reasons: merging is strictly better than not, because before this phase no state writer had a leak refusal at all and now five do; and adding a refusal to three live operator paths at phase close is a behaviour change that deserves its own wave and its own tests rather than a patch written against a deadline. The honest reading is that the leak class is narrowed, not closed, and the three rows this phase closed for it name the paths they actually covered.

**The bake that this phase performed is sound, which was checked rather than assumed.** The guard that walks a release to BAKED iterates the required targets and would be vacuously true if a configuration declared none, which is a real defect. It did not apply to `0.7.0.dev2`: the checkpoint renders from the train template, and the template marks all three targets required, so both guards iterated three ids and each leg reached observed success through the one verb that writes that status. The defect is held off today by configuration data rather than by code, which is why it is carried as a major.

**The majors are carried, not repaired.** The sharpest is that the `changes` job's own failure skips the test matrix, and a job skipped for an unmet dependency reports as skipped, which a required status check reads as passing - so a classifier crash could present a green required check with zero tests executed. That did not happen on this phase's pull request: the classifier passed, the diff touches source, and both matrix legs ran the suite. Its sibling is that the classifier treats every path under the state document and the typed stores as changing nothing the heavy jobs exercise, while a committed census test reads exactly those paths - so a state-only push skips the one test that would have judged it. The rest, in brief: a git-ref registry reader that runs without an explicit working directory, reintroducing the working-directory dependence this phase removed from the readiness sweep; a frozen manifest bound to a version but not to the source commit pinned beside it; two npm read-back paths that settle an unreadable or partial answer as a conclusive mismatch rather than as unknown; conformance certification writing caller-authored capability statuses verbatim rather than binding them to the probe verdicts; quarantine leaving the last-known-good pin's pre-quarantine certification readable; output metering omitting cache-creation tokens from the total the in-flight cap is tested against; ambient tool denials rendered into two lanes' native configuration namespaces under names those lanes do not use, so the denial matches nothing; roughly twenty ledger appends committing durable mutations outside the seven-step transaction this phase established, filed as B158; a torn ledger tail that boot recovery can never reach on a tree whose native traffic writes no journal record, filed as B157; and a workspace-global sequence allocated per root, so two roots serving one workspace can issue the same number.

**One cadence fact belongs in the record because a reader will otherwise misread the green.** The twice-green job is skipped on every pull request by design under the cadence this phase shipped; it runs after merge, on the push to the default branch. So this phase's pull-request green contains no twice-green proof, and a flake that job would have caught surfaces after the merge rather than before it.

## What the follow-up iter recovered

The phase closed once, and then its own pull request red on a platform leg, which is the ordering the close-timing rule exists to catch: the macOS matrix leg failed twice on two real-daemon tests whose spawn budget is five seconds. `P33-I02` was opened for the repair and holds one wave.

**The cause was measured before anything was changed.** Handlers register by import side effect, and the daemon server imported all thirty-nine method modules before it could answer anything. Cold daemon import had grown from 0.60s at the phase base to 0.84s on the branch, and almost all of the growth is Pydantic building validators for models a liveness probe never touches. The ceiling of the fix was checked first, by deleting those imports and re-measuring, so the work was known to be worth doing before it was written.

**The repair spends less rather than raising the ceiling.** The five-second budget is what stops an operator hanging on a daemon that will never answer, so widening it to quiet a test would have spent the user's margin to hide a regression. The server now imports the liveness module alone; the subscribe modules register through names it already reads, and every other verb registers when the registry first misses. Enumerating the surface loads it too, so nothing that asks for the whole method list gets a partial answer.

**Measured after: 0.49s, below the phase base rather than merely level with it.** The eager registry holds eight methods at startup against the full two hundred and five. The whole tree passes at 25,746 tests with the type checker clean, and the two tests that red in CI pass locally.

Three things about this wave are worth recording because they are the kind of thing that passes review quietly. The formatter removed the eager registration imports as unused, which would have left the optimisation defeated while every test still passed; the import is now single and carries the suppression that says why it exists. The first coverage test written for it asserted that every file in the package appears in the module list, which was wrong - several entry modules import helpers that register verbs of their own - so it now asserts that every name a decorator registers anywhere in the package resolves, which is the property that matters. And the test tier lint refused a unit-tier test that spawns a child interpreter, so the out-of-process probes moved to the integration tier, where proving a fresh interpreter's import behaviour belongs.

This wave, like the one before it, closed with zero gate receipts on evidence produced by hand rather than by the close machinery.

**Its criteria are gated, but weakly, and the reason is worth recording.** The iter close refused at first: the determinism ratio scores what fraction of gating criteria a deterministic oracle falsifies, and four prose criteria carrying no tier scored zero against a floor of 0.80. The armed flag and the drain path are both ratified - a prior closeout rejected lowering the flag because it lowers a gate the repository deliberately armed, and named the legacy-conversion verb as the way to drain untyped rows. Running it typed all four at the static tier. What it produced, though, is a single-word source grep per criterion, so a criterion asserting that dispatch resolves a deferred verb is now gated by whether one word from its own sentence appears in the wave's files. That is deterministic and it is not discriminating, which puts these four in the same family as the four gates named above. The behavioural evidence is the committed test module and the before-and-after measurements, not the gate. The gap is that the tooling offers no supported way to bind a real command gate to a wave authored at phase close: the conversion verb emits pattern gates, and the gate-rewrite verb touches only argv and refuses a kind change.

## What did not land

**The `0.7.0.dev3` cut.** `release create` for a rung declaring membership requires `membership_refs` naming an ACCEPTED Milestone acceptance bundle. The canary rehearsal accepted no Milestone and could not, because the acceptance verb reads a `PendingAction` record that nothing in epoch 2 writes. The canary's own evidence file parses with a `milestones` array of length zero, so there is no reference a dev3 record could honestly name. The trap is that `release create` validates only that `membership_refs` is non-empty: a plausible but fabricated reference would pass it and fail much later at preflight as `MEMBERSHIP_UNRESOLVED`, after a DRAFT record had been opened, a version bumped and a changelog section written for a checkpoint that cannot prove its own membership. Backing that out costs more than not doing it, so the cut defers to P34. The version module stays at `0.7.0.dev2` and no changelog section was opened; the tag-chokepoint suite passing unchanged is the evidence that not bumping was the correct reading rather than an omission.

**The console launcher.** The console is a real package with thirty-one of its thirty-four routes bound to native read models and tested, and it has no production entry point: every construction site in `src/` is test tooling passing a loaded fixture and a fake clock, and none of the three declared entry points starts it. Filed as B153, with B154 for the epoch-1 retirement it implies and B155 for the idle contract that would have made the gap red a gate instead of a report.

**The train advance.** Advancing moves the derived open rung onto dev3, which no dev3 record can occupy, so the gain would have been cosmetic while the price was waiving a full-suite gate on a real release record. Declined for the same reason the fabricated membership reference was declined.

## Verification integrity

**Eleven of dev2's twelve gates are receipted at the pinned source.** The twelfth, `epoch1_stabilization`, earned no receipt: the live-runtime-directory guard cannot attribute the change it detects, and every concurrent `eawf` invocation - including `--help` - auto-spawns the daemon and trips it, so no amount of operator discipline produces the receipt while the guard stands.

**The reported drift count understates the real one, and the mechanism matters more than the number.** `eawf status` reports twenty-five drifts, all of them P32 waves whose pins that phase's rebase merge invalidated. All one hundred P33 pins likewise point at pre-squash commits that are not ancestors of the head, and they are not reported because the derivation scans every ref rather than the branch, and sixty-eight local-only refs - eleven per-wave branches and fifty-seven agent branches, left behind when their worktrees were torn down - still carry the pre-squash lineage. Those refs exist on no remote and on no other machine. The honest post-merge figure is therefore one hundred and twenty-five, and P32's own closeout audit promised the pin refresh that would have cleared its twenty-five, which never ran. The pin refresh belongs immediately after this merge, before the keeper refs are deleted, and the deletion should follow it rather than precede it.

**Three backlog rows shipped without being closed, and are closed at this audit.** B149, B150 and B151 describe the three leak-hygiene repairs, and W06, W07 and W08 shipped exactly them - the first wave's title is the row's title verbatim, and each wave description opens with its row id. All three waves closed with pinned commits while all three rows stayed open with no resolution. This is the bookkeeping counterpart of the producerless-seam pattern: the work landed and the record that says so did not.

**The squash is verified rather than asserted.** The phase's 135 commits collapse to nine thematic clusters plus one state-bookkeeping commit, carrying all one hundred wave trailers between them; the pre- and post-squash trees diff empty and share one root tree object. The collapse needed git plumbing because the commit lint has no satisfiable state for a phase-close squash - carrying the trailers fails the closed-wave rule, dropping them fails the trailer-required rule, and there is no third option. The lint runs only at the commit-message stage and has no CI counterpart, so nothing downstream re-checks it, but a rule set with no satisfiable state for a routine operation has a hole in it.
